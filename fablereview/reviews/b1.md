## What Astra did well

- The regression harness asserts *behavior*, not "a call happened": hidden IDs, absence of the secret title in the serialized JSON (`checks/incident-detail-regression.mjs:102`), the three retained positive exceptions (admin / service-matched responder `u-bob` / assignee `u-alice`), the direct-request denial, `readCalls === 9`, and `persistCalls === 0` / `writeCalls === 0` plus snapshot equality to prove the filter is read-only.

## One-paragraph summary for the index
This is one of the cleanest lessons in the batch: a real confidentiality bug in a Next.js incident-response API, fixed by reusing the app's own `canViewIncident` policy at the point where related records are embedded in a detail response. The regression harness genuinely proves the behavior — hidden IDs, absent secret titles, retained admin/responder/assignee exceptions, and a proof that the filter never mutates the store — and VERIFICATION.md is candid about what mocked storage does and does not show. The weaknesses are shape, not substance: heavy unbroken prose, one solution leaking into the practice file, and a very small diff for 4000 words. Do it if you want to internalize "authorize every record you return, not just the one that was asked for" — budget about two hours.
