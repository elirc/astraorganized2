## What Astra did well
- The change is a genuine, non-cosmetic CRUD/reliability fix. `CheckoutService.cs:37-83` now captures one clock value, calls `Order.ValidateCustomer` and `product.ValidatePurchase` before `CreatePaymentIntentAsync`, and constructs the `Order` *before* `ReserveInventory`/`MarkRedeemed`. The backup shows the original mutated stock and redeemed the coupon before `Order.CreatePending` could throw — a real "side effects survive a rejected request" bug.
- The tests are real xUnit tests exercising the change, not call-counting theater alone. They assert **state**: `product.Variants.Single().StockQuantity.Should().Be(2)`, `coupon.RedeemedCount.Should().Be(0)`, `(await dbContext.Orders.CountAsync()).Should().Be(0)` — plus counters on the three external boundaries. They use the *real* `ProductRepository`/`OrderRepository` over EF InMemory rather than mocking the repository interfaces, so `Include`/value-object mapping stays in the path (justified in `04-TESTING-AND-DEBUGGING.md` para 1).
- Idempotency is taught honestly. `03-WORKED-CHANGE.md` explicitly says the early return "is application-level idempotency for the observed terminal state; it does not replace an idempotency key for two simultaneous pending requests", and that the post-await recheck "cannot undo a provider intent". That is the correct ASP.NET/EF framing — a junior is not told a preflight is a transaction.
- Limits are specific and true: EF InMemory does not enforce relational constraints or isolation; the fake gateway is not a processor; the pre-existing unauthenticated seller-mutation endpoints and ID-only download are called out rather than papered over.

## Problems found (evidence required)
- **[D6]** The delivery silently repaired a **compile-breaking** line. Backup `src/Netgum.Domain/ValueObjects/EmailAddress.cs:30` reads `throw new DomainException("Email address is not valid.") from ex;` — `raise ... from` is Python, not C#; the solution could not have built before this pass. The evidence summary euphemises this as "converts malformed email parsing into the expected DomainException contract", and no course chapter mentions it. A learner reading `02-CONCEPTS.md` ("`Order.ValidateCustomer` runs the actual `EmailAddress.Create` parser") will not know the parser was previously unbuildable. This also means the "no regressions" framing rests on a baseline that never ran.
- **[D5]** `VERIFICATION.md` gives net8 commands but the only recorded execution used `DOTNET_ROLL_FORWARD=Major` on a net10 runtime, and the log's results path is Astra's stage dir (`astra-remaining-improvements\stage\028086674b89\...`), not the delivered folder. It is disclosed, but a junior on a machine with only .NET 10 will hit the same gap and the course never says "install the .NET 8 SDK from <x>" or pins an SDK in `global.json`.
- **[D3]** There is no explicit predict-before-run prompt. `05-PRACTICE.md` exercises are well scoped ("Exercise 3: unavailable variants") but phrased as "write a test that…", not "predict the counter values, then run". The failure-diagnosis material in `04` para 4 is good but is prose, not a lab the learner breaks and fixes.

## One-paragraph summary for the index
This is one of the better courses in the batch and a good model for the "validate before you touch anything external" lesson. It teaches, on real ASP.NET/EF Core code, why deterministic validation must precede a payment-gateway call and why a tracked EF entity must not be mutated before the aggregate you are about to save is known to be constructible. The tests are honest xUnit tests that assert persisted and tracked state plus external-call counters, using the project's real repositories over EF InMemory, and the evidence (TRX, log, on-disk test count) all agree at 11/11. Its main weakness is a silent prerequisite repair — the original `EmailAddress.cs` had a Python-style `throw ... from ex` and could not compile — which the course never mentions, plus a lack of explicit predict-then-run prompts. Do this one early in the .NET track; budget two to three sessions.
